# License and commercial use policy

**Source-available, not OSI-open-source.** The initial project's purpose is to let the community study, learn, experiment and share noncommercial improvements, while leaving commercial exploitation subject to permission from the relevant rightsholders.

## Original software

Unless explicitly marked otherwise, original AI Game Arena software is made available under the [PolyForm Noncommercial License 1.0.0](LICENSE). It allows noncommercial use, modification and redistribution under its exact conditions. Commercial use is not licensed by default; operators of a paid hosted service, companies embedding our original code in commercial products, and others requiring commercial rights must negotiate a separate agreement with the relevant rightsholders.

**The official full text controls.** In particular, the license itself includes provisions for specific organizations, including educational institutions and charities; this page does not narrow those permissions.

This license protects proprietary monetization more directly than AGPL. AGPL would require relevant source publication in certain cases, but **does not prohibit commercial use or profits**. PolyForm Noncommercial is therefore a deliberate choice that sacrifices OSI-approved open-source status.

## Original documentation

Except where otherwise specified, original documentation, including this project's planning and design specifications, is licensed under [CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 International](LICENSE-DOCS.md). The official legal code controls.

## Third-party dependencies and borrowed code

We cannot relicense code we do not own. A file with an upstream MIT/BSD/GPL license keeps that license, its notices and all applicable conditions. Our PolyForm terms apply **only to new, independently licensable original code**, not to vendored or copied upstream components.

- MIT/BSD components may often be dependencies or reused with their original notices, but they themselves remain available under their upstream licenses.
- GPLv2 OpenTTD components cannot simply be relicensed under PolyForm. Review whether any planned combination or distribution constitutes a derivative/combined work, and follow the relevant GPL obligations.
- Joost's `OpenTTD LLM
 project lacked a clearly verified explicit permission grant during initial review: **no code may be copied until permission is established**. Studying the public architectural concepts is different from copying protected source.
- Prefer separation by process and APIs where appropriate, but **process separation is not automatic legal proof** of license compatibility.

Track decisions and provenance in [THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md](THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md).

## Contributions and dual licensing

Contributors retain copyright in their submissions. A noncommercial license from a contributor does **not automatically** give us permission to issue commercial licenses for their work. Before accepting contributions that may enter a commercially licensed version, obtain an explicit written contribution agreement (e.g. an appropriate CLA) or a separate assignment/grant.

Until that process is in place, accept design discussions, bug reports and tests first; resolve rights before merging substantial externally authored code. See [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md).

## Permissions, marks, and warranty

The repository owner may negotiate commercial use of **the code they own**, but not third-party content. The project name and branding are not automatically licensed for misleading endorsement. The license contains the software warranty disclaimer.

For commercial permission, open a GitHub issue without disclosing confidential business information, then arrange a written agreement outside GitHub.

This is a project policy, not tailored legal advice. Have qualified counsel review the licensing structure before accepting significant contributions or launching commercial distribution.
